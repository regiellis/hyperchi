The first map of the archipelago had thirty islands. It looked wonderful on
paper and played like a spreadsheet. The final map has eleven.

## Every island needs a reason

We gave each island one rule that changes how you dock there. Hollin Pier has a
crane that takes heavy cargo. Skerry Low only has water at high tide. Brannock
will not take passengers after midnight. If we could not think of a rule, the
island went.

## Distance is the real design tool

The space between islands matters more than the islands. Short hops let you
fix a mistake the same night; long ones make you commit. We moved islands
around for weeks, measuring crossings in lamp oil rather than in kilometers.

## Drawing the map

The map in the wheelhouse is drawn as if the captain made it: pencil, a few
coffee rings, corrections in a second hand. It updates as you learn the
currents, so the map you finish with is a record of your winter.
