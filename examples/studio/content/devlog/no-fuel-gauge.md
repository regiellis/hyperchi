title: Why the ferry has no fuel gauge
date: 2025-11-12
author: Mara Ilves
tags: design

summary: We removed the oil gauge from The Lamplit Crossing and the game got better. Here is what replaced it.
---

For most of last year the *Marguerite* had a fuel gauge in the corner of the
screen. Players watched it the way you watch a phone battery: constantly, and
with a low hum of dread. They stopped looking at the sea.

## What the gauge was doing

We logged where players looked during a voyage. More than half of the time
went to the gauge and the route timer. The islands, the weather, and the
passengers, the parts we had spent two years drawing, got the rest.

The gauge also made every decision arithmetic. Players calculated the cheapest
route and took it. Nobody detoured to Skerry Low for a stranded fisherman,
because the gauge said not to.

## What replaced it

The lanterns themselves. Each lantern on deck burns down visibly, and the
light it throws on the water shrinks as it does. When oil runs low you see
less of the sea, which is both the warning and the cost.

## What we lost

Precision. A few testers asked for the number back, so it lives in the
accessibility menu as an exact readout. Most people never turn it on.
